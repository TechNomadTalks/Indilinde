About

A mountain that teaches.

Indilinde is a community-built project led by The Green Net NPC with Enhlanhleni Development NPO — not a luxury lodge, but development that starts with the land and the people who already live on it.

High above kwaNguza, in the hills behind Umzumbe on the KwaZulu-Natal south coast, Indilinde is becoming a place where people learn from the land, from each other, and from the cultural knowledge of the community.

Indilinde is a community-based project built around ecotourism, environmental education, cultural heritage and practical skills, led by The Green Net NPC with Enhlanhleni Development NPO.

We are not building a luxury lodge, and we are not in a hurry. We are building development that starts with the land and the people who already live on it, and that leaves both stronger.

What makes this different

A network, not a hierarchy.

The Green Net NPC was launched on 29 February 2020 by a small group of South Coast residents, including Helen Dodge, who had already spent decades teaching, writing, and working for environmental and social upliftment in KwaZulu-Natal. From the start, it was designed to be different from the typical top-down NGO.

Network, not hierarchy

Most South African NGOs operate through a central organisation that directs projects. The Green Net works as a network — connecting people, projects, and markets across the Ugu region, letting initiatives grow from the ground up rather than being imposed from above.

Interconnection over single issues

Many organisations focus on one thing — recycling, or gardening, or education. The Green Net weaves these together. A food garden feeds families and teaches soil science. A craft workshop preserves culture and creates income. Nothing sits in a silo.

Community-led, not community-facing

The Green Net does not run projects "for" communities. It supports projects that communities run themselves. Indilinde is owned by Enhlanhleni Development NPO. The Green Net is the catalyst and partner, not the owner.

Practical, not performative

No luxury lodge. No donor-driven photo ops. The Green Net measures success in compost bins built, seedlings distributed, workshops completed, and families feeding themselves. That is why Indilinde is taking its time — because it is being built properly.

Teaching, not telling

Helen Dodge spent 11 years as a lecturer at Vista University Soweto, teaching English literature and discourse studies. That experience shaped The Green Net's approach: knowledge passes hand to hand, workshops replace lectures, and every lesson is something you can take home and use.

Art as a way in

Helen is also an independent artist — her "Fabulous flowers" series celebrates South African orchids, irises, and strelitzias. At Indilinde, art is not decoration. It is a teaching tool, a cultural bridge, and a way of seeing the land that pure data cannot reach.

"We hold a vision of a world in complete balance and harmony, in which humanity nurtures and enhances our natural environment, treating all life with respect. A safety Net for the planet."
— The Green Net NPC vision statement

Teacher · Artist · Founder

Helen Dodge

Helen did not arrive at The Green Net by accident. She arrived through teaching, through art, and through a long conviction that environmental work must be rooted in community and culture — not imposed from outside.

1985 – 1996

Teacher, Vista University Soweto

Lecturer in English literature and discourse studies. Taught English for business purposes, essay-writing, and cultural studies. Organised cultural days and course-related outings. Built the teaching materials that later became the backbone of Indilinde's learning approach.

1990s – present

Artist & writer

Independent painter, writer, and editor. Known for "Fabulous flowers" — large, bold paintings of South African indigenous flowers: African irises, strelitzias, Disperis orchids, tibouchina, and coral flowers. Paints yoga postures and psycho-spiritual imagery. Arts reportage and environmental journalism.

2000s – 2020

Environmental organiser

Founded The Green Net NPC, launched 29 February 2020. Organised community food gardens during Covid-19 lockdown, distributing seedlings and composters across Louisiana and Tweni. Opposed offshore oil drilling. Produced the KZN Biodiversity Calendar highlighting indigenous species.

2020 – present

Indilinde founder

Purchased the kwaNguza mountain plot in April 2025. Building Indilinde as a place where teaching, art, craft, and conservation meet — not as separate programmes, but as one growing thing. The mountain is the classroom. The community is the teacher.

"The one way to help people ensure their own food security in the long term, rather than relying on 'lockdown' food parcels, is to re-ignite a culture of home food gardening."
— Helen Dodge, on the community garden initiative, 2020
